Edit those, not this file: `node --test scripts/qa/rating.test.js` fails when this file is out of date. The page's Methodology tab shows the same content from the engine output (`ratingDoc.method`). Research and data behind it: [sources.md](sources.md), [data-map.md](data-map.md); interface: [engine-contract.md](engine-contract.md).

## What this is

This rating scores every office of Malda Zone (the zone, its 3 regions, 7 divisions and 41 CCCs) on one 100-mark scale and grades it from A+ to D, the way India rates its power distribution companies.

It is modelled on the two national ratings run for the Ministry of Power: REC's Consumer Service Rating of DISCOMs (CSRD) and PFC's Integrated Rating. The Ministry's Distribution Utilities Ranking combines the two. CEA sits on the rating committee and sets some of the definitions, but publishes no rating of its own.

The national ratings score whole companies; here they are adapted to offices, so only what an office controls is scored. From CSRD come its four consumer-service pillars (reliability; connection services; metering and billing; faults and grievances) and its grade bands. From PFC and the RDSS scheme comes a fifth pillar, loss and revenue: AT&C loss, distribution loss against the office's target, collection efficiency, receivables and the year-on-year trend.

Company finance is left out: the ACS-ARR gap, debt service, liquidity, subsidy, tariff and accounts items depend on the tariff, power purchase and the state, not on an office.

Every parameter is scored as both national ratings do it: full marks at or beyond a stated benchmark, no marks at or beyond a stated floor, in proportion in between. The benchmarks are RDSS, WBERC, CSRD or PFC values wherever one exists. Where none exists for an office-level measure, the scale is a zone choice and is marked as one.

Each office is computed from its own totals (a division's AT&C loss comes from the division's own input, billing and collection), never as an average of its children, and it is ranked only against offices of its own level.

**Scoring:** marks = maximum × (value − zero-marks level) ÷ (full-marks level − zero-marks level), kept between 0 and the maximum. The pillar marks add up to 100; penalties are taken off; the score is kept between 0 and 100.

## Grade bands

| Grade | Score | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| A+ | 90 and above | Excellent |
| A | 80 to under 90 | Very good |
| B+ | 70 to under 80 | Good |
| B | 60 to under 70 | Fair |
| C+ | 50 to under 60 | Below average |
| C | 40 to under 50 | Poor |
| D | under 40 | Very poor |

The CSRD grade bands, identical in all five CSRD editions. The PFC Integrated Rating uses other names (B-, C-) and its D means a loan default, so those are not used. Source: REC CSRD FY 2023-24 p.73 and FY 2024-25 PDF p.37.

Inside each pillar the parameter marks add up to the pillar weight. The national ratings weigh reliability most (CSRD 45). Here it gets 15, because the data behind it is the thinnest: no consumers per feeder, no DT failure register, and CCCs only through a name match. Connection services get more (20 against 10) because those lists are complete and current at every level.

## Parameters

"Full" is the value that earns all the marks, "zero" the value that earns none; in between, marks are in proportion. Levels: where the parameter can be measured (elsewhere it is n/a, see below).

### Loss & revenue (30)

| Parameter | Formula | Full | Zero | Marks | Source | Levels |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|
| AT&C loss | 100 × (input − min(collection, billed)) ÷ input, MU, 12 months to the latest month | 15 % | 25 % | 10 | RDSS | zone, region, division, ccc |
| Distribution loss vs the office target | distribution loss ÷ the office's target distribution loss (loss_targets) | 1 × | 1.2 × | 6 | PFC Integrated Rating (14th) | zone, region, division, ccc |
| Collection efficiency | 100 × collection ÷ billed, MU (capped at 100 as in the loss sheet) | 99.5 % | 91 % | 6 | PFC Integrated Rating (14th) | zone, region, division, ccc |
| Receivables | OSD of live consumers ÷ average daily collection over 12 months (LT; HT collections at regions excluded) | 60 days | 120 days | 5 | PFC Integrated Rating (14th) | zone, region, division, ccc |
| AT&C loss trend on a year earlier | 100 × (AT&C now − AT&C a year earlier) ÷ AT&C a year earlier | -5 % | 0 % | 3 | PFC Integrated Rating (14th) | zone, region, division, ccc |

- **AT&C loss.** RDSS 2.1.1 (AT&C 12-15 % target); 3.2.2.2 (divisions above 25 % are priority loss areas). Data: Loss report (`loss_report`, /loss.html), Loss targets (`loss_targets`, /loss.html).
- **Distribution loss vs the office target.** IR14 B1: actual ÷ approved loss, ≤ 1 full, > 1.2 zero; the office target is WBSEDCL's (loss_targets). Data: Loss report (`loss_report`, /loss.html), Loss targets (`loss_targets`, /loss.html).
- **Collection efficiency.** IR14 B3 absolute scale (≥ 99.5 % full, < 91 % zero); RDSS 3.1.5.4(xv) national target ≥ 98 %. Data: Loss report (`loss_report`, /loss.html).
- **Receivables.** IR14 A2 days receivable (≤ 60 full, > 120 zero), on collection instead of revenue. Data: Consumers (`consumers`, /consumer/consumer.html), Collection report (`collection_report`, /collection.html).
- **AT&C loss trend on a year earlier.** IR14 trajectory marks (1-year improvement better than 5 %); RDSS REM 4.12.8 (no marks if the same or worse). Data: Loss report (`loss_report`, /loss.html).

## Safety penalties

Safety works like the PFC disincentives: marks are taken off, never added, up to 5 in all.

Fatal electrical accidents to people in 12 months per lakh live consumers: none costs nothing, 5 or more per lakh costs 4 marks, in proportion in between. Unsafe sites found by the safety app and not rectified: 10 % or less costs nothing, 50 % or more costs 1 mark.

A penalty is not applied when its register is stale, so missing data can never improve a score, and every office is treated alike. The scorecard then says safety was not assessed.

| Penalty | Formula | No deduction at | Full deduction at | Marks | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| Fatal electrical accidents | fatal accidents to people in 12 months ÷ live consumers × 1,00,000 | 0 per lakh consumers | 5 per lakh consumers | −4 | Modelled on IR14 Pillar D (capped disincentives); scale: zone choice, one fatal accident in an average CCC (55,000 consumers) costs about 1.5 marks |
| Unsafe sites not rectified | 100 − share of unsafe sites found in safety inspections that are rectified | 10 % | 50 % | −1 | Modelled on IR14 Pillar D; scale: zone choice |
| **Cap** | | | | **−5** | |

## Levels: regions, divisions and CCCs

Every office is scored on its own summed inputs: the rows of all its CCCs plus any rows booked at the office itself. The formula is applied to those totals; children's scores are never averaged.

Regions carry HT input and collections booked at the region office that no division has, so a region's loss and collection figures are not the sum of its divisions. Regions are scored on their full totals including HT, divisions and CCCs on theirs. The scorecard marks every value that includes such rows.

Ranks are within a level: CCCs among the 41 CCCs, divisions among the 7, regions among the 3. The zone has a headline score and grade only.

For a parent's weak parameters (under half marks), the scorecard shows each child's excess over the full-marks level in real units (MU, applications, meters, ₹ lakh), which add up to the parent's excess, and each child's share of it.

## When a parameter cannot be measured

A parameter that cannot be measured at an office is n/a there and never counts as zero: the source has no column for that level (feeder outages and CMO grievances below region), the office has no cases on the list, too few cases to judge, too little feeder coverage, or the data is stale.

Its marks go to the other parameters of the same pillar in proportion to their marks, so the pillar keeps its weight. If nothing in a pillar can be measured, the pillar's weight goes to the other pillars in proportion to their weights. The scorecard says where this happened.

An office needs at least 60 of the 100 base marks measurable to be graded and ranked.

## Data quality

Each input is checked before it is scored, and numbers are never filled in.

- Stale (older than the metric's freshness limit on the day of the run): excluded until the source is refreshed, and re-weighted as above.
- Too few cases (under the metric's minimum, e.g. 20 pending master cards or 10 PM Surya Ghar installations): excluded for that office.
- CCC reliability where under 60 % of the CCC's DTs sit on feeders matched to the outage log: excluded for that CCC.
- Known caveats that do not change the number (a mixed second dump that is not used, a copy two days behind its sheet, a replaced year-earlier month): scored, with the caveat shown next to the value.

## Ranks, "why this grade" and "what would move it up"

Offices are ranked by score within their level, highest first. Equal scores (to 2 decimals) share a rank, and the next rank is skipped (1, 2, 2, 4).

"Why this grade" names the band, the strongest and weakest pillars, every parameter at zero, and everything n/a. "What would move it up" lists the parameters with the most marks to gain, the value that earns full marks, and, where one parameter alone can do it, the value that reaches the next grade.

## This run's data caveats

- HT input booked at region offices: Malda 313 MU, Raiganj 334 MU, Balurghat 78 MU (725 MU, 19 % of the zone's input). Regions include it; no division or CCC carries it. HT collections booked at regions are left out of receivables at every level, because the OSD sheet has no HT rows.
- The loss report's month a year earlier has misplaced CCC rows for Bamongola, Old Malda, Tapan, Kumarganj (pairs swapped within their division). For these CCCs the AT&C trend uses the median of that month and its two neighbours as the base. Division and higher figures are not affected.
- Reliability is a proxy. The feeder outage log has no CCC column, so each 11 kV feeder is tied to the DTs it feeds through the DT list (substation and feeder name): 33,802 of 40,429 DTs (84 %) are covered. Availability is the outage hours a DT saw, weighted by DTs (WBERC weights by consumers; DTs stand in for them). Under 60 % coverage, reliability is not scored: Aiho, Pandua, Gangarampur. Interruptions per feeder are counted for divisions and above only. Agricultural feeders are not yet left out, as WBERC's SAIFI/SAIDI would.
- Stale inputs, not scored or applied until refreshed: Fatal electrical accidents (people) in 12 months (as on 2025-06-21); Unsafe sites found by the safety app that are rectified (as on 2026-03-10).
- Inputs are as on different dates: loss, consumers and receivables to the end of the latest loss month, pending lists in the last days before the run, feeder outages to the latest report month. Each value shows its own date.

## How this compares with the old performance index

The old index scored six parameters (AT&C loss 30, pending NSC count 25, master cards over 3 months 20, connection delay 9, quotation and work-order delay 8 each) and scaled each one between the best and the worst office of the level, so the best office always got full marks and the worst none, whatever their actual performance. Why they differ:

- **Absolute standards, not best-versus-worst.** A parameter now earns marks against a fixed benchmark (RDSS, WBERC, CSRD, PFC), so an office can improve its marks without another office getting worse, and a level where every office is weak (load extensions pending beyond 90 days) gives everyone few marks instead of full marks to the least bad.
- **Shares and rates instead of raw counts.** The old index gave 45 of 100 to the size of the pending NSC and master-card lists, so big offices lost marks for being big (Kaliyaganj, Gazole and Raiganj CCCs, with 146-374 pending applications) and small ones gained (Patiram, Buniadpur, Manikchak). The new rating uses the share pending beyond the WBERC timeline and rates per 10,000 consumers.
- **Five pillars instead of two.** Reliability, metering and billing, receivables, collection, and faults and grievances were not in the old index. Balurghat and Buniadpur divisions led it on short pending lists; with 1,121 and 1,143 open dockets and the lowest supply availability (97.6 %) they now rank 4th and 6th. Sujapur, Kaliachak and Samsi rose on few connections overdue beyond 30 days, full or nearly full reliability marks and few open fault dockets; Patiram and Kusmandi fell on supply availability and open fault dockets, Manikchak on overdue connections, receivables and defective meters.
- **Missing data is not perfect data.** The old index counted an office with no loss row as 0 % AT&C loss (full marks) and an empty list as the best value. Here a missing, stale, too-small or unmatched input is n/a and its weight is shared, so it neither costs nor earns marks.

## Left out, and why

| Item | Reason |
|---|---|
| ACS-ARR gap, days payable, quick ratio, DSCR, debt to assets (PFC Financial Sustainability, 75 marks) | Company accounts: power purchase, borrowing and tariff are decided at head office and by WBERC. |
| Subsidy realised, loss takeover, government dues, tariff cycle, fuel pass-through (PFC External Environment) | Decided by the state government and WBERC. |
| PFC disincentives (audit opinion, accounts, defaults, governance, regulatory assets) | Statutory accounts and lenders: company level. |
| CSRD regulation alignment, predetermined charges, tariff categories, monthly domestic billing | Set by WBERC regulations and company policy. |
| CSRD call centre coverage and waiting time, grievance forums | Run centrally for the whole company. |
| CSRD bills on actual reading, non-manual reading, SMS coverage, prepaid share, digital payment | Office-level, but no office-level data exists yet. |
| CSRD DT failure rate, hours of supply by consumer type | No DT failure register and no consumers per feeder; reliability uses the feeder outage log instead. |
| Rooftop solar applications not connected 30 days after the application | The 30-day clock in the Rules starts at the installation certificate; the rating scores the office's step (net metering after installation) instead. |

## Data that would make it better

- A DT failure register (DT code, date failed, date replaced) and a feeder → CCC table would bring the CSRD DT failure rate and direct CCC reliability.
- A monthly dump of released connections with payment and release dates would score time to connect as CSRD does (the historical NSC list stops in March 2026).
- A closed-docket dump with created and closed times would score fault restoration time against WBERC Reg 9.1 (docket closure times stop in June 2025).
- Billing in rupees per CCC would measure receivables against revenue as PFC does, rather than against collection.
- Consecutive monthly defective-meter summaries would score meter replacement, not just the backlog.
- Fresh accident and safety-app registers would let the safety penalties apply.
